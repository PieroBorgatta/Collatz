    The purpose of this note is to point out that a stronger statement than the one requested has
been available since 1999. Garcia and Tal [1] proved that every infinite orbit of T has Banach
density zero, by a pigeonhole argument over parity classes. Their theorem, made quantitative,
gives a power-saving count of orbit points in every window, uniformly in the window position
(Theorem 2.3 below, for which we give a short self-contained proof with an explicit exponent).
From it: the reciprocal sum of a divergent orbit converges (Proposition 3.1), which is the
harmonic-thinness requirement of [5] in the strongest possible form; the floor exclusion then
holds for every B ≤ 1 (Theorem 4.1); and a windowed occupation bound pushes it to every
B < 1/β ∗ = 1.03585 . . . (Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.3), past the harmonic barrier B = 1 that
no packing argument over distinct states can cross.
    Everything here is elementary. The only nonclassical ingredient is the observation of [1]
that a windowed pigeonhole over parity classes applies to a single injective orbit; the counting
that feeds it goes back to Terras [2] and Everett [3]. The exponent β ∗ = rH log2 3, where rH
is the critical ones-ratio of Rozier [4], arises from the same entropy equation as his constant,
which is reassuring rather than surprising: both express the tradeoff between the number of
parity prefixes of a given tilt and the contraction they force.
    Scope. Nothing below constrains hypothetical nontrivial cycles, proves any tier of the
Effective Occupation Conjecture of [5], or approaches the Collatz conjecture. The contribution
is the identification of [1] as the missing input to the occupation program, the explicit exponent,
and the threshold 1/β ∗ .

5 Limitations
The exponent β ∗ is a genuine barrier for the argument of Theorem 2.3: it balances the entropy
of heavy parity prefixes against the contraction of light ones, and both sides are sharp for the
hypothesis class. Improving 1/β ∗ therefore requires either deeper iteration than N ≈ log2 X
inside the window argument, which breaks Lemma 2.1’s modulus bookkeeping, or an input
beyond parity counting. Nothing here constrains nontrivial cycles: a periodic orbit is not
aperiodic, so it lies outside every hypothesis above, and the theorems concern divergence
candidates only, exactly as in [5, Remark 7.4]. Nothing here bears on the sub-critical occupation
of convergent orbits, which is the open core of the Effective Occupation Conjecture: in the
language of [6], the anti-concentration problem for the residue classes of near-critical words is
untouched.
